Business Analysis Training case study

Bright Bank: Digital Banking Reliability & Transaction Status

A retail bank faced rising complaints about downtime and failed, delayed or unclear transactions. I defined the problem, mapped stakeholder needs, wrote the requirements and recommended a solution.

View Full Breakdown Download CV

  1. Business problem

    Customers could not rely on digital banking

    Bright Bank was receiving a growing number of complaints from customers who could not access their accounts, make transfers or complete payments during periods of system downtime. Transactions were failing, staying pending or returning unclear error messages, so customers could not tell whether money had been deducted.

    The Customer Support Team was spending significant time handling complaints, and management was concerned about customer satisfaction, trust, operational efficiency and the bank’s reputation.

  2. Objective

    Reliable services and clear information

    Understand the root of the problem, identify what each stakeholder group needs, and define a solution that improves the reliability and availability of Bright Bank’s digital banking services and gives customers clear information about their transactions.

  3. Approach

    A structured business analysis workflow

    I worked through the case using a standard BA sequence:

    • Problem identification
    • Stakeholder identification and needs
    • Business requirements
    • Functional requirements
    • Non-functional requirements
    • Solution recommendation

  4. Analysis

    Six stakeholder groups, three levels of requirements

    The core business problem was failed, delayed and pending transactions on the mobile and online platforms, combined with unclear failure messages. I then defined what each stakeholder group needed:

    StakeholderWhat they need
    CustomersReliable, fast transactions and clear status: successful, pending, failed or reversed, and whether money was deducted
    Customer SupportAccurate, accessible transaction information to resolve issues quickly
    Bank ManagementFewer failures and complaints, higher satisfaction and visibility of digital performance
    IT / TechnologyMonitoring tools, transaction logs and system alerts to fix problems fast
    Operations / PaymentsAccurate processing and status information for reconciliation
    Compliance / RiskAccurate records, controls, security and audit trails

    From these needs I wrote eight business requirements, three functional requirements (transaction status tracking, clear customer notifications and a Customer Support investigation tool) and three non-functional requirements (performance, reliability and security).

  5. Findings

    Uncertainty was the real pain point

    • Customers’ biggest frustration was not knowing whether a transaction had succeeded, failed or been reversed, and whether money had left their account.
    • Customer Support lacked quick access to accurate transaction information, which slowed resolution and increased workload.
    • Management and IT lacked visibility of transaction problems, making root causes harder to spot and fix.

  6. Recommendations

    A Digital Transaction Monitoring and Status Management System

    • Connect a monitoring system to the mobile app, online banking and payment-processing systems, tracking every transaction from start to completion and flagging failed, delayed or pending ones.
    • Send customers clear status notifications: Successful, Pending, Failed or Reversed/Refunded.
    • Give authorised Support staff a dashboard to search by transaction ID, account, date or amount and see status, history, errors and whether the account was debited or refunded.
    • Address the root causes of failures rather than just adding a new system.
    Example notification: “Transaction Failed: Your transfer was unsuccessful. No money was deducted from your account.”

  7. Outcome

    A complete requirements-led solution

    A full business analysis solution covering problem identification, stakeholder needs, business, functional and non-functional requirements, and a recommended solution with clear success measures: transaction failure rates, processing times, complaint volumes, support calls and customer satisfaction before and after implementation.

  8. Business impact

    Expected benefits

    This was a training case study, so there is no measured real-world impact.
    • Fewer failed and delayed transactions
    • Clearer information for customers and less confusion about deducted funds
    • Fewer complaints and a lighter Customer Support workload
    • Faster problem detection for IT
    • Higher customer satisfaction and trust
